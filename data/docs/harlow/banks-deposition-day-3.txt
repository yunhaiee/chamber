Harlow Orchards v. Brightline Freight, CGC-26-418822
Deposition of Theo Banks (Brightline dispatcher), Day 3. Notes by M. Chen.

Started at 9:20, thirty minutes late because the court reporter's laptop wouldn't connect. Opposing counsel (Alan Pierce) objected to form on nearly every question. None of it matters.

Banks admitted the refrigeration unit on trailer 4742 showed a temperature alarm at 01:40 on September 26, and that nobody on the night shift acknowledged it until 04:00. The witness said the alarm log "gets ignored a lot" because of false alarms.

Banks confirmed Brightline's policy requires a driver call within 30 minutes of any alarm, and could not name anyone who made that call.

Banks estimated the load at 1,433 cases of apricots for Harlow Orchards, and said Brightline's claims team valued the loss at 48,800. Ours is 61,400. Flag for the damages expert.

On the email chain, Banks said a supervisor, Alice Quinn, said "don't write anything down about the alarm." Pierce instructed the witness not to answer follow-ups. We should move to compel.

Lunch ran long. The afternoon was mostly exhibits we already had.
