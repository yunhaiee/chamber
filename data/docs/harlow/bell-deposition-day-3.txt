Harlow Orchards v. Brightline Freight, CGC-26-418822
Deposition of Marcus Bell (Brightline night supervisor), Day 3. Notes by M. Chen.

Started at 9:05, fifteen minutes late because the court reporter's laptop wouldn't connect. Opposing counsel (Alan Pierce) objected to form on nearly every question. None of it matters.

Bell admitted the refrigeration unit on trailer 4348 showed a temperature alarm at 03:05 on 17 June, and that nobody on the night shift acknowledged it until 06:00. The witness said the alarm log "gets ignored a lot" because of false alarms.

Bell confirmed Brightline's policy requires a driver call within 30 minutes of any alarm, and could not name anyone who made that call.

Bell estimated the load at 912 cases of plums for Harlow Orchards, and said Brightline's claims team valued the loss at 50,600. Ours is 70,800. Flag for the damages expert.

On the email chain, Bell said a supervisor, Rosa Delgado, said "don't write anything down about the alarm." Pierce instructed the witness not to answer follow-ups. We should move to compel.

Lunch ran long. The afternoon was mostly exhibits we already had.
