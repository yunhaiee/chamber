Harlow Orchards v. Brightline Freight, CGC-26-418822
Deposition of Dana Kowalski (Brightline claims adjuster), Day 1. Notes by M. Chen.

Started at 9:05, fifteen minutes late because the conference room was double booked. Opposing counsel (Alan Pierce) objected to form on nearly every question. None of it matters.

Kowalski admitted the refrigeration unit on trailer 4367 showed a temperature alarm at 04:15 on 14 Sep, and that nobody on the night shift acknowledged it until 07:30. The witness said the alarm log "gets ignored a lot" because of false alarms.

Kowalski confirmed Brightline's policy requires a driver call within 30 minutes of any alarm, and could not name anyone who made that call.

Kowalski estimated the load at 1,225 cases of apples for Sunvale Foods, and said Brightline's claims team valued the loss at 68,100. Ours is 84,200. Flag for the damages expert.

On the email chain, Kowalski said the supervisor, Glenn Ashby, told the witness "don't write anything down about the alarm." Pierce instructed the witness not to answer follow-ups. We should move to compel.

We broke twice for phone calls. The last hour covered background we already knew.
