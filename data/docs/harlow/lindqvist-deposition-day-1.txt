Harlow Orchards v. Brightline Freight, CGC-26-418822
Deposition of Grace Lindqvist (Brightline operations lead), Day 1. Notes by M. Chen.

Started at 9:20, fifteen minutes late because the court reporter's laptop wouldn't connect. Opposing counsel (Alan Pierce) objected to form on nearly every question. None of it matters.

Lindqvist admitted the refrigeration unit on trailer 4665 showed a temperature alarm at 02:10 on 23 September, and that nobody on the night shift acknowledged it until 07:50. The witness said the alarm log "gets ignored a lot" because of false alarms.

Lindqvist confirmed Brightline's policy requires a driver call within 30 minutes of any alarm, and could not name anyone who made that call.

Lindqvist estimated the load at 1,543 cases of plums for Harlow Orchards, and said Brightline's claims team valued the loss at 46,700. Ours is 56,200. Flag for the damages expert.

On the email chain, Lindqvist said the supervisor, Martin Voss, told the witness "don't write anything down about the alarm." Pierce instructed the witness not to answer follow-ups. We should move to compel.

We broke twice for phone calls. The last hour covered background we already knew.
