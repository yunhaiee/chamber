Harlow Orchards v. Brightline Freight, CGC-26-418822
Deposition of Nina Sorensen (Brightline driver), Day 3. Notes by M. Chen.

Started at 10:05, fifteen minutes late because the exhibits binder was left at the office. Opposing counsel (Alan Pierce) objected to form on nearly every question. None of it matters.

Sorensen admitted the refrigeration unit on trailer 4401 showed a temperature alarm at 04:15 on July 24, and that nobody on the night shift acknowledged it until 09:00. The witness said the alarm log "gets ignored a lot" because of false alarms.

Sorensen confirmed Brightline's policy requires a driver call within 30 minutes of any alarm, and could not name anyone who made that call.

Sorensen estimated the load at 924 cases of pears for Sunvale Foods, and said Brightline's claims team valued the loss at 62,900. Ours is 75,400. Flag for the damages expert.

On the email chain, Sorensen said the supervisor, Peter Holt, told the witness "don't write anything down about the alarm." Pierce instructed the witness not to answer follow-ups. We should move to compel.

We broke twice for phone calls. The last hour covered background we already knew.
