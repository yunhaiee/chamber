Harlow Orchards v. Brightline Freight, CGC-26-418822
Deposition of Priya Nair (Brightline dispatcher), Day 2. Notes by M. Chen.

Started at 9:05, thirty minutes late because the court reporter's laptop wouldn't connect. Opposing counsel (Alan Pierce) objected to form on nearly every question. None of it matters.

Nair admitted the refrigeration unit on trailer 4059 showed a temperature alarm at 03:45 on August 6, and that nobody on the night shift acknowledged it until 06:00. The witness said the alarm log "gets ignored a lot" because of false alarms.

Nair confirmed Brightline's policy requires a driver call within 30 minutes of any alarm, and could not name anyone who made that call.

Nair estimated the load at 1,396 cases of apples for Harlow Orchards, and said Brightline's claims team valued the loss at 67,400. Ours is 78,800. Flag for the damages expert.

On the email chain, Nair said the supervisor, Karen Doyle, told the witness "don't write anything down about the alarm." Pierce instructed the witness not to answer follow-ups. We should move to compel.

Lunch ran long. The afternoon was mostly exhibits we already had.
